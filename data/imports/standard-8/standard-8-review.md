# Standard 8 — Financial management

The Health Service Organisation at all levels of the Papua New Guinea health system strengthen their financial mechanisms and processes by which they access and utilise public resources from pools at national, provincial and district levels. There will need to improve accountability for the delivery of health services across all levels of health services.

Function 1; Component 1 (Leadership & Governance).

## 8.1 Policies and guidelines

### 8.1.1

The Senior Executive Management Team (SEM) of the Health Service Organisation and PHA Finance and Budget Committee members, all Section 32 officers, requisitioning officers and financial delegates, have read and understood the relevant sections of various Acts that are referred to in the Public Finance Manual. These include;
A copy of the PFM Act 1995, PFM (Amendment) Act 2016, PFM (Amendment) Act 2018
Financial Management Manual (2012 and new copy when it is released by Department of Finance)
All Financial Regulations, Instructions and Circulars issued by Central Agencies (but also any that are issued by Provincial Administrations as well),
The Section 32 Officer Learner’s Manual (2014)
The National Procurement Act
The Audit Act 1989
The National Audit Manual
The Public Service Management Act 2014
The General Orders

1. Documents (a) to (i) have been circulated and read, understood, by the relevant officers as evidenced by the initialling of the relevant officer after reading.

## 8.2 The chief executive officer (CEO) and the governing board ensure compliance with the enforcement of national public financial management (PFM) legislation and guidelines

### 8.2.1

The CEO and Board of Governance ensure compliance with Central Ministry instructions to implement effective PFM and its accountability requirements to national and provincial governments.

1. Circulars issued by Department of Treasury (DoT), Department of Finance (DoF), Department of National Planning and Monitoring (DNPM), Department of Personal Management (DPM), key PHA Corporate officers and divisional managers are read and implemented (see initialling after reading).
2. There is evidence of compliance with the national budget timetable, reporting timelines and quarterly reviews (review reports).

### 8.2.2

There is a credible budget planning process where budget and revenue estimates align to organisational resource needs and strategic service plans.

1. Evidence that budget estimates are prepared with input from district health managers, directors, hospital CEOs, hospital management and corporate staff (see meeting minutes, emails).
2. Evidence that budget estimates are relevant and against a checklist.
3. Evidence that final annual budget and forward year expenditure and revenue estimates align to provincial service delivery plans (see costed annual implementation plans), approved by the PHA board.
4. Evidence of annual implementation plans and procurement plans prepared in detail by major cost centres (provincial and district hospitals, CSS, public health and curative health divisions and by district health managers).

### 8.2.3

There is a credible budget implementation process across the organisation.

1. Evidence of monthly cash flow forecasts for all cost centres are reviewed and submitted through the Board of Governance and CEO to Central Ministries (see meeting minutes/ reports).
2. Evidence of dedicated Finance team follow up on weekly and monthly warrant authorities and cash disbursements anticipated from Central Ministries (see reports).
3. The Finance and Budget Committee ensure that cash disbursements and reprioritisations of cash/payments are conducted to fulfil the requirement to keep health facilities open and operational (see meeting minutes and reports).
4. Quarterly financial performance reviews are sent to the Board of Governance (see reports).

### 8.2.4

The Health Care Organisation has internal budget controls in place to ensure the accountable usage of public funds to the organisation.

1. Evidence of CEO’s delegation of authority to Account Officers to authorise payments and collect revenues on behalf of the organisation. (see letter of authorisation).
2. Evidence of delegated authority to financial delegates to provide advice and implement spending decisions of requisition officers. (see letter of authorisation).
3. Evidence of delegation of authority to Section 32 officers to approve requisitions in line with the specified financial limits across operational budgets, maintenance, capital purchases and capital works. (see letter of authorisation).

### 8.2.5

The Health Care Organisation have strong working relationships with Central Ministries to improve alignment of approved budget estimates with service delivery plans and disbursement of cash with implementation requirements.

1. The CEO and Corporate Director have the contact details of senior officers in DNPM, DoT and DoF (evidence of work email addresses and numbers).
2. Evidence of regular meetings or correspondence with these agencies and DPM key officers. (see email correspondences or meeting minutes)
3. Evidence of follow-up on budget submissions, warrant authorities, cash disbursements, procurement processes and HR processes. (see email correspondences).

### 8.2.6

The Health Care Organisation has strong working relationships with Provincial and District Administrations to improve provincial resourcing of primary and rural health care.

1. Evidence of communication with Provincial and District Administration (see email or meeting minutes).

### 8.2.7

The Health Service Organisation has established a fully functional Finance and Budget Committee to ensure that the formulation, oversight and reporting on the organisation’s budget is conducted in a proactive way and is compliant with national requirements.

1. Evidence of a Terms of Reference for the Finance and Budget Committee approved by SEM and Board of Governance.
2. There is a scheduled timetable for Finance and Budget Committee meetings (see timetable).
3. Minutes of Committee meetings are escalated to the Board of Governance and CEO for review.
4. Committee meet on budget formulation and budget implementation (see meeting agenda)
5. Circulars, instructions and internal memos issued through the Committee are sent to the rest of the organisation (see email or circulars).

### 8.2.8

The Health Service Organisation has established a fully functional Project Steering Committee (PSC), established to direct and oversee both planning and the development of health infrastructure across the organisation.

1. There are Terms of Reference for the Project Steering Committee.
2. Meeting minutes of the PSC demonstrate discussion on the development health infrastructure.
3. Reports from the PSC meetings are reported to the Board of Governance.

### 8.2.9

The Health Service Organisation has established a fully functional Audit & Risk Management Committee established to make effective use of the internal audit function.

1. There are Terms of Reference developed for the Committee and approved by SEM and Board of Governance.
2. Evidence of meeting schedule set up for the Committee annually.
3. Meeting minutes indicate discussions on risk management, governance and internal control systems for the organisation.

## 8.3 PHA board, chief executive officer and senior executive managers accountability mechanisms

### 8.3.1

Public Finance Management accountability requirements of the CEO and Board of Governance are fully understood and followed, to ensure the efficient and transparent usage of public and other resources to improve health outcomes in the province.
The CEOs must know their mandated role as the Chief Accountable Officer and the PFM powers that they command, who they are responsible to report to on overall PFM and performance reporting, in what format, how often to report and what to do with the reports in terms of strategic decision making before the Board.
The CEOs must also know their role in enforcing accountability requirements across all other officers working in the PHA in meeting mandatory performance objectives of the PHA and ensuring the effective management of its day-to-day operations.

1. Evidence that the CEO is aware of his role in PFM, reporting structures, format of reporting and the use of financial reports (interview).

### 8.3.2

The Health Service Organisation reviews quarterly financial performance and financial statements for the Board of Governance.

1. Evidence of quarterly reviews of financial performance (see reports)
2. Internal quarterly Integrated Financial Management Systems (IFMS) reports are prepared by the Corporate Director and the Finance Committee, for presentation to Finance Committee and PHA board

### 8.3.3

The Board of Governance approves budget estimates and annual financial statements.

1. Evidence of Board approval for budget estimates.
2. Evidence of Board approval for annual financial statements.

## 8.4 Health financing management plan (supported by legislation, policies and internal control measures)

### 8.4.1

The Health Service Organisation has an annual budget timeline developed, an internal budget process and annual budget cycle requirements.

1. There is an annual internal timetable by the Finance Committee for responsible officers to submit to plan for mandatory quarterly internal reporting.

### 8.4.2

The Health Service Organisation Fee structure has been established and gazetted to keep track of the revenues that it generates, such as hospital user fees and other medical charges.

1. There is a gazetted fee structure with;
specific charges to designated services across different facility levels.
(b)any services to be provided free of charge.
2. User Fees and No Fees Structures are displayed on Signboards within Health Facility outpatient and patient services waiting area.
3. User Fees and No Fees Structures are announce publicly and regularly, daily.

### 8.4.3

Provincial and district consultations are conducted after the budget is passed by Parliament and approved by Provincial Assemblies

1. There are consultations between the financial team and Provincial and District administrations to ensure that income receipts are provided by Provincial and District Administrations, in line with forecasted requirements (evidence of meeting minutes/reports).
2. Evidence of discussion for potential delays in receipts of income for internal reprioritisation of available funds for specific pre-approved purposes and commitments (evidence of communication/ emails/reports)

## 8.5 Budget & financial management (day to day procedures)

### 8.5.1

The Health Service Organisation maintains an operating account. There should be effective usage and maintenance of this account.
There is an internal operating account manual with the following criteria;
No one individual 
oversees identifying a spending need
is ensuring it is in line with the organisation’s plan for the year and delivers value for money (i.e. proper procurement guidelines have been followed).
does verification of both budget appropriation and funds which are available through warrant authority and CFCs.
making a commitment.
reviewing the commitment to ensure it is in line with financial procedural requirements
administering the transaction
carrying out a review of the transaction

1. There is an internal operating accounts manual/guideline.
2. The manual highlights (a) to (g).

### 8.5.2

The Health Service Organisation maintains a Health Services Improvement Project (HSIP) trust account. The HSIP staff must effectively manage the trust account by adhering to;
The HSIP Trust Instrument
The Trust Account Manual of procedures
The Public Finances Management Act (PFMA)
The DPM General Orders, and
Any memorandum of understanding between the Development Partners and the Government of PNG.

1. Evidence of documents (a) to (e).
2. There is a designated staff managing the HSIP account.

### 8.5.3

Bank reconciliations are conducted weekly to ensure that the Health Service Organisations’ underlying financial records are accurate and reliable.

1. Evidence of weekly and regular bank reconciliations.
2. Evidence that the bank reconciliation report is updated in IFMS.
3. Reconciliation of cash book entries and bank statement entries conducted.
4. Evidence that bank reconciliations are prepared regularly by Public Bodies and submitted to DoF 14 days after the end of each month.
5. End of year financial accounts and statements are prepared.

### 8.5.4

Budget adjustment requests are tracked and reported.
There should be summaries of requisition requests and Section 32 approvals that require the redistribution of cash funds or actual budget adjustments in IFMS/ the accounting system used by the organisation.
There should be Board approved guidelines that specify the conditions under which budget adjustments within and across expenditure votes can take place and these guidelines are circulated to;
Section 32 officers
Financial Delegates
Commitment Clerks
Authorizing Officers and Certifying Officers, Directors of Public and Curative Health
the heads of the Church and Catholic Health Services District health managers and other budget holders

1. Evidence that accounts officers maintain summaries of requests for budget adjustments (see summaries).
2. There is evidence of Board approved guidelines for budget adjustments.
3. The guidelines should be circulated to (a) to (e). (see circulation list)

## 8.6 Budget & financial management audit reporting

### 8.6.1

Annual reports should be prepared for publication to meet PFM Act, PHA Act and Audit Act requirements

1. Performance and management plans are prepared annually for submission to DoF (see plans).
2. Financial statements are prepared before the end of each financial year, in a format specified by Finance and in line with the Audit Act 1989 (inspect financial statements)
3. An annual report is sent to the Minister for Health and the Governor of the Province on the performance of its functions and duties under the PHA Act (see report).
4. The annual report is expected to be submitted by the 31st of May against the previous year’s calendar year performance (see date of submission).

Training and support are provided by Central Agencies and NDoH throughout the year.

Training and capacity building work delivered through donor partner programmes.
