# Standard 15 — Supportive supervision

All Healthcare Organisations advocate for Provincial and District Hospital Department Heads and District Health Managers-Coordinators, as well as working with the Public Health team, to manage, implement, monitor & report results of an integrated supervision approach that reflects continuous quality improvement.
The Provincial and District Department Heads as well as District Health Managers, monitor the performance report of each urban and/or rural health facility, district and hospital division/department at Quarterly Review Meetings;
A Hospital and Urban-Rural Health Service Supervision Checklist and Performance Framework is utilised.

Function 2; Component 1 (Leadership & Governance).

## 15.1 Provincial and district hospital dept managers and district health managers for urban and rural health services in partnership with public health program managers and officers undertake supervision visits and report results

### 15.1.1

Multi-Disciplinary approach from all Clinicians Managers and Supervisors of the health organization towards daily operations, supervision and productivity of health sector employees.

1. Multi-disciplinary approach to planning, leading, and organising, supervising staff, to make informed decisions and accept responsibility for improving the quality of health services provided.
This includes using jusdgement over effective use of resources, information technology, performance and productivity of the health facility workforce

### 15.1.2

Supportive supervision promotes efficient, effective and equitable health care and fosters improvements in the procedures, personal interactions, and management of all hospitals and primary health care services.

1. Improved supervisory competencies of supervisors at both the provincial and district levels to ensure that regular supervisory visits are conducted in the districts.
2. There is a minimum of four (4) supervisory visits to each health facility each year.
3. Utilising the relevant checklist for the provincial and district hospitals and Levels 1 – 4 facilities, i.e., rural to urban health services.

## 15.2 Use of hospital integrated supervision checklist and urban-rural health services supervision checklist

### 15.2.1

Good supervision results in good leadership, empowerment, guidance, coaching, motivation, self-discipline and direction with a view to developing the productivity, performance, satisfaction, fulfilment and the knowledge and skills of the health worker and the worker’s commitments to the goals of the organisation.

1. Evidence of an established and approved annual Hospital and Public Health Integrated Supervision Plan   for urban-rural health services prepared by Department Heads, DHMs and OICs.
2. Senior managers and health staff of hospital and public health Programs attendance at education or awareness sessions on;
approved Hospital and Public Health program Integrated Supervision Annual plan, its purpose and benefits to staff and team.  
How to use both the hospital and urban-rural health services supervision checklist.

### 15.2.2

During supervisory visit, Supervisors follow nine (9)    Supervisory Steps, i.e.;
Observations of Department / Clinical Unit (physical environment)
Full Staff (Team) Meeting
Review all the evidence / data: i.e.: Registers, NHIS tablet, ANC cards, medical records, critical incident reports.
Discuss within the team, relevant Elements and Components, fill out an initial score.
Conduct “Brainstorming” with the full team to summarise agreed actions and recommendations to be taken for each component within each relevant element.
Complete each Element Summary, scores, and calculate percentages; Each element summary provides a total score /% as well the summary of agreed actions & recommendations.
Complete onsite in-service training: based on identified topics or priority topics from the staff (team) meeting.
Complete the 'Supervision Performance Scorecard & Summary Report' (front sheet summary) and distribute copies to Hospital Dept/Division/Unit Heads/Clinical Directors/Public Health Unit/OICs and other relevant officers.
Provide Feedback and plan for the next supervisory visit.

1. Evidence availablle at hospital and public health programs health facilities   confirms  Supervisors complaince with the nine (9) Supervisory Steps during the Supervisory Integrated Visit.

## 15.3 PHA manager accountabilities

### 15.3.1

The health organisation holds individual Department Heads, DHMs and OICs of heath programs/facilities accountable for these Key Deliverable’s;
Deliverable 1: Hospital Dept Heads /Public Health Program Managers / DHMs carry out;
Supportive supervision visits at least 4 times a year
Full multidisciplinary team meeting
Deliverable 2: Hospital Dept Heads /Public Health Program Managers /DHMs presents at Quarterly review meetings;
Supervision results
Identified agenda item /activity for further action. 
Deliverable 3: Quality Coordinator (QC) & Internal Auditor (IA) compile all results & key issues & recommendations for Corporate & Clinical Governance (incl. Public Health) Committee meetings.
Deliverable 4: Corporate & Clinical Governance Committee meetings prioritise key recommendations to go to SEMT, supported by QC & IA; CG & CG meeting have standard agenda item for results from supervision. SEM confirm for CEO to Board.
Deliverable 5: CEO Provides  Overall Report  to Board of Governance on;
Supervision Performance Scorecard .
Summary of Significant Issues/Recommendations.

1. Evidence of supportive visits, with reports, at least 4 times per year, as well as meeting minutes of the full multi-disciplinary team held quarterly.
2. Hospital Dept Heads /Public Health Program Managers / DHMs carry out;
Supportive supervision visits at least 4 times a year
Full multidisciplinary team meeting
3. Evidence of Integrated  Supervisory Visits Statement  Document  on   Key Issues & recommendations   prepared by  Quality Coordinator (QC) & Internal Auditor (IA)  presentation to  Corporate & Clinical Governance (incl. Public Health) Committee meetings.
4. Evidence that SEMT received  Integrated Supervisory Visits Statement  Document  on Priorities Key Issues & recommendations from Corporate & Clinical Governance Committee

## 15.4 Supervision performance and reporting

### 15.4.1

The CEO of Health Organization ensures that;
Quarterly SEMT meeting to present and discuss, cross check written quarterly Supervision Framework report against Key Health Deliverables and Targets. 
Finalise Supervision report and submit to Board of Governance.

1. Evidence that All health facilities at that PHA are delivering services in accordance with designated level of care, NHS standards, and have access to drugs and equipment required.
2. Evidence of a functioning network of health facilities are open and providing services in accordance with designated levels (NHSS).
3. Evidence of signed Service Level Agreements (SLAs) with churches and targets for all health facilities Level 2 to Level 4 are being implemented.
4. The organisation has implemented SLA and is managing with all health facilities receive a minimum of four supportive supervisory visit annually using relevant supervisory tools for hospitals, rural health services and public health.
5. The L5 Provincial Hospital undergoes self-appraisal using relevant supervisory tools at a minimum of 2 assessment annually.
6. Evidence that agreed targets for all NHIS health indicators for each health facilities are being achieved.

### 15.4.2

Results of supervision visits are monitored against eNHIS data and other health date collection systems.

1. Evidence that supervisory tools are being used.
2. Evidence that supervisory visits are completed and analysed at each review period of, at minimum, 6 months
3. Evidence that reporting is through the monthly hospital divisional units, allied health and rural health services on agreed indicators.
