# Standard 6 — Medical supplies procurement and supply chain management

The Healthcare Organisation ensures drugs and medical supplies are available in the ‘right quantity, quality and right price’ in all health facilities consistent with the level of care and services provided by that facility. All health facilities are to ensure that drugs and medical supplies in general, are adequately stored in conditions that maintain the quality (efficacy) of the products as recommended by the manufacturers of the products, and that any out-of-date stock are dealt with appropriately.

Function 1; Component 4 (Environment).

## 6.1 Requisitions for stock replenishment are based on accurate demand quantification

### 6.1.1

The demand for every item that is stored in a health facility is based on documented past usage data sourced from stock records, and matched where possible with actual and forecast morbidity data for each item.

1. There is evidence of accurate usage data from stock records against actual and forecast morbidity data. (see records and data).

## 6.2 Supply planning is undertaken to facilitate quantified demand

### 6.2.1

The supply requisitioning process is done methodically and ensuring that supply meets accurately estimated demand for each item. Supply planning should have;
Stock currently in hand minus any expired stock- in- hand
Duration of stock based on average monthly usage data 
Duration of stock- in- hand and stock- on -order but not received yet.

1. Evidence of supply planning criteria prior to requisition of supplies. (interview)
2. Evidence of the use of M- supply system to monitor stock status. (interview and observation)
3. Evidence of consultations with programme managers on annual programme stock (HIV, Malaria, TB, Vaccines) (see emails or meeting minutes).

## 6.3 Storage conditions and practices do not adversely impact the quality and efficacy of drugs & stored medical supplies

### 6.3.1

Storage practices should be consistent with national criteria for storage and store keeping practices.
Storage conditions should meet manufacturers’ requirements to maintain drugs and medical supplies quality. This includes the following aspects of storage management;
Quality of products is affected by high temperature, high humidity, lack of ventilation and exposure to pests and vermin (e.g. rats)
Incorrect labelling 
Expiry dates and products past expiry 
Methodical storage of products in boxes and shelves
Accountability for stock: all receipts and issues are recorded in a stock register and periodic stock- takes carried out to check whether physically available quantities match quantities in stock register. 
There is a designated officer managing the store

1. There is a designated officer in charge of the store.
2. Evidence of stock accountability: see stock register for receipt and issue records.
3. Evidence of periodic stock- takes to match physical stocks and stocks on register (see reports).
4. The floor layout, shelving and space for pallets are adequate for the optimal storage of medical supplies.
5. Evidence that items that need refrigerated/cold room conditions have adequate, well managed space and there is a constant supply of electricity (AC and DC current via a standby generator).
6. Evidence that there is a demarcated area to store empty and filled medical gas cylinders.
7. Evidence of FEFO (First to Expire First to be issued) practice for all products with a manufacturer specified shelf life.

## 6.4 Methodical stock management to ensure a stock balance where there is no under- stocking or over- stocking

### 6.4.1

Quantities requisitioned periodically should be to meet accurately estimated demand and not to create excess stock of some and under- stocking of others.
The Store should be making sure the stock level does not exceed the Maximum Stock Level and does not fall below the Minimum Stock Level. The following are considered:
Average Monthly Usage
Lead Time 
Minimum Stock Level 
Maximum Stock Level

1. Evidence of documentation of average monthly use, lead time, minimum and maximum stock levels.
2. Evidence of maintenance of stock levels between Minimum Stock Level and Maximum Stock Levels for each item.

## 6.5 Product expiry management

### 6.5.1

Avoid the amount of stock that expires in the store prior to being used. Ensure that;
Expired stock is not issued to any patient.
Products that expire first issued first – First to Expire, First Out (FEFO)
Stock that has already expired should not be counted as stock in hand
All expired stock is removed from shelves and disposed in accordance with NDOH’s expired stock disposal procedures.

1. Evidence that product expiry management procedures are being utilised.
2. Evidence of appropriate expired stock disposal.

## 6.6 Medical supply chain management

### 6.6.1

The Organisation undertakes periodic supply planning in line with the strategic direction identified in the 10-year Master Plan for Medical Supply Chain Management. This would include;
The stock of drugs & medical supplies in its health facilities is provincial stock with stock transferability between health facilities. 
Geographic, seasonal, logistics (road conditions included) and other situations that make stock transfers difficult or impractical noted.
Procuring/requisitioning frequency reduced and,  order quantities increased where logistics difficulties impact on delivery lead times.
A provincial buffer stock of essential items introduced and buffer stock holdings in individual health facilities reduced.

1. Evidence of an Organisational drugs and medical supplies chain master plan.

## 6.7 Annual procurement planning

### 6.7.1

Evidence that the Organisation carries out an Annual Procurement Planning identifying:
The estimated quantity of drugs and medical supply items required for the upcoming year.
The budget required to meet the cost of this estimated need 
Required delivery time frames.
The exercise is carried out by the 31st of October every year and sent to the NDOH Medical Supply Procurement & Distribution Branch (MSPD) during the first week of November every year.

1. Evidence of an annual procurement planning exercise (meeting minutes or reports) within the set annual time frames.
2. Evidence of budgetary planning for the estimated needs (reports)
3. Evidence that procurement of drugs and medical supplies is consistent with the latest available NDOH Medical Supply Catalogue.

## 6.8 Distribution planning

### 6.8.1

The Organisation undertakes distribution planning, 
management of drugs, medical supplies, vaccines, medical gases and programme stock in accordance with the 10- Year Master Plan for Medical Supply Chain Management. This includes;
The method, time, frequency and cost for transportation to health facilities.
Partnerships for distribution
Transit Stores may be carried out by distribution contractors chosen through a competitive tender process contract.

1. Evidence of policy or guidelines for medical supplies distribution.
2. Evidence of a tender process that awards distribution contracts to qualified tenderers.
3. Evidence of safety measures that are included in the tender and the contract to ensure the quality of goods is safeguarded by the contractor/s during transportation.

## 6.9 Purchase order management

### 6.9.1

Ensure that purchase order management is carried out efficiently and that shortcomings in management does not contribute to supply shortages, payment delays to suppliers, and ad hoc emergency procurement.

1. Evidence of a comprehensive data base for purchase order management (including expected delivery dates, actual delivery dates, alerts that signal receipt delays, reconciling with delivery invoices and supplier performance assessments).
2. Evidence of supplier performance assessments being carried out and becoming a criterion for procurement evaluations. (see reports)

## 6.10 Timely payments to suppliers

### 6.10.1

Ensure there is adherence to agreed terms and conditions with suppliers and there are no negative procurement outcomes due to payment delays to suppliers.

1. Evidence that there is a clear indication of payment terms in invitations to bid for anticipated procurement of drugs and medical supplies (see the invitation to bid).
2. Evidence of a clear indication of supplier’s terms and conditions in their bids (see example of payment bids).

## 6.11 Warehousing

### 6.11.1

Ensure that the warehouse layout facilitates the efficient and effective movement, with accountable storage of goods that are stored in a warehouse. There should be adequate space for;
incoming goods
a holding area to keep goods until they are stored
an area to store medical gases and cylinders
a cold room for vaccines
refrigerators for items requiring storage in such conditions
a safe and secure area for dangerous and controlled drugs
an area to keep goods that are picked and ready for dispatch
a separate area to keep damaged/expired items until they are disposed according to policy requirements

1. Evidence of appropriate layout, shelves, pallets and handling equipment (observation).
2. Evidence of appropriate space with all the necessary storage areas (observation).
3. Evidence of the smooth movements through the warehouse, including incoming goods, customer orders (requisitions), inter-facility transfers, dispatches, and returns (observation, interviews).

## 6.12 Efficient receiving of goods

### 6.12.1

There should be efficient receiving of goods consistent with terms and conditions in the relevant purchase orders in the warehouse. The goods should be moved to a holding area as soon as possible and to minimise any inconvenience and/or delays to shippers who deliver goods.

1. Evidence of a procedure to check whether goods received conform to the terms and conditions specified in the purchase (see SOP, observe, interview)
2. Evidence that the receiving area is cleared of goods received as soon as possible and goods are moved to a holding area (see SOP, observe, interview).
3. Evidence that there is a supervised procedure in place to check quantities received as per delivery notes and against quantities (see SOP, observe, interview)
4. Evidence information in the Logistics Management Information System (LMIS)/ m-Supply is entered promptly and to convey information and documentation to the procuring entity. (check the LMIS)

## 6.13 Effective and efficient picking and packing operation

### 6.13.1

Ensure that the picking and packing operation in the warehouse is: 
planned, methodical and efficient
carried out by maximising the labour input
done accurately under supervision 
stock picked and packed are in accordance with the relevant picking slips generated by m-Supply 
all pickings are confirmed in the m-Supply with the least possible delay
The picking schedule should account for at least 80% of items to be picked and packed that week, leaving a maximum of 20% for unscheduled, urgent picking and packing of items that may be required.

1. Evidence of preparation of a weekly picking schedule for the warehouse by the Warehouse Manager (see schedule).
2. Evidence of all items picked are moved to the packing area as soon as possible and packed ready for dispatch in clearly labelled boxes/containers (observation).
3. Evidence of accurate entries made in m-Supply or equivalent, and entries confirmed by a supervisor (see m-Supply entry).
