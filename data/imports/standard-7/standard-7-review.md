# Standard 7 — Physical infrastructure, environment management and safety protocols

The infrastructure for a Healthcare Organisation is developed based on population, disease burden and geography.  Environmental Management Standards are implemented to support the safety and maintenance of buildings and the surrounding areas; including walkways, plant and other equipment, medical devices, the management of its supplies and consumables, utilities including electricity and water, and workplace design, signage and fire safety
The organisation’s management of its assets, goods and general services is an aspect of its responsibility to ensure the safety of consumers / patients and guardians, staff, contractors and other visitors and to manage risk.

Function 1; Component 4 (Environment).

## 7.1 Planning, design and safety

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The design and infrastructure of the Health Service Organisation must reflect the commitment to a safe practice and a safe environment while also providing for consumers / patients and guardians with diverse needs.

### 7.1.1

The planning and design of health facilities and its refurbishment must be compliant with relevant legislations with safety features ensured.

1. The design of the building conforms to relevant building codes and regulations.

### 7.1.2

There is planning and management of internal roads with respect to: 
drop-off and collection of patients/clients
pedestrian safety
ambulance movement
client/consumer/patient transport,
movement of vehicles through the grounds,
speed of vehicles moving through the facility
appropriate parking areas which are safe

1. Visual inspection of (a) to (g) shows evidence of appropriate internal roads for the facility.

### 7.1.3

The planning and management of walkways within the facility is adequate with respect to;
width
trip hazards
disabled access
movement of clients/consumers 
movement of patient
safety of staff and visitors

1. Visual inspection of (a) to (g) shows evidence of appropriate walkways for the facility.

### 7.1.4

All plants and other permanent fixtures are chosen for safety and efficiency and installed and maintained by qualified individuals and as per the manufacturers’ instructions.

1. The plant and permanent fixtures are maintained by a qualified individual (see credentials and job description).
2. The plant and permanent fixtures are safe for use (visual inspection).
3. Permanent fixtures are installed according to manufacturers’ instructions (review example of instructions and fixture).

### 7.1.5

The Health Facility has completed the L3-5 Rapid Health Facility Assessment Tool.

1. Evidence of Completion of the Rapid Assessment Tool.

### 7.1.6

There should be clear, well-located internal and external signages to meet the needs of consumers / patients, visitors and staff.

1. Evidence that signages and directions are visible and clear to access services.

### 7.1.7

Disability access and facilities meet legislative requirements, which are based on recognised guidelines and is appropriate to the needs of the community and the organisation.

1. Evidence of disability appropriate signage which includes the use of multilingual / international symbols (should be height appropriate, have tactile features, pictograms).
2. Presence of accessible toilets with signage.
3. Presence of ramps and adequate space in areas catering to disability services

### 7.1.8

There is a critical incident monitoring system for risks associated with the health facility environment, these risks are investigated, reported and remedial actions taken.
There should be a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for staff, patients, guardians and other visitors to report incidents relating to buildings, roads, walkways, plant, medical devices, equipment, consumables and supplies.

1. Evidence of a SOP to report incidents relating to buildings, roads, walkways, plant, medical devices, equipment, consumables and supplies.
2. Evidence that staff (clinical and non-clinical) know where to access this SOP (interview).

## 7.2 Fire safety (mandatory standard)

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The Health Service Organisation and individual clinical and non-clinical units must comply with all fire safety standards. Department/Unit Managers/OICs and staff are all aware of compliance with statutory requirements (building code and/or fire standards) and recommendations from the fire brigade (if available). This includes planning of new buildings to meet standards.

### 7.2.1

The health facility infrastructure complies with building codes for fire safety.

1. Evidence of compliance to building codes for fire safety.

### 7.2.2

The local fire brigade performs annual inspections to identify risks.

1. Annual fire safety inspection report from the local fire brigade.
2. Evidence that the organisation takes action to implement the recommendations from the local fire brigade.

### 7.2.3

The fire extinguishers within the facility should fulfil the following criteria:
are in appropriate locations
have a current service tag
have fire hoses
is recorded on the asset registry
is frequently checked

1. Evidence that the fire hoses / extinguishers are in the appropriate locations in the areas of work (observe).
2. Evidence that extinguishers have current service tags.
3. Evidence that the fire extinguisher is listed on the asset register.
4. Evidence that fire hoses are checked at least quarterly (documented record).

### 7.2.4

Department/Unit Managers/OICs and staff ensure all fire exits are always kept free of obstruction and fire exit doors open from inside and are not locked from inside.

1. Fire exits are free from obstruction.
2. Fire exit doors open from the inside and is not locked.

### 7.2.5

Department/Unit Managers/OICs and team are trained in the use of fire extinguishing equipment at least annually, in emergency fire response, and understand fire alarm systems / panels around the hospital.

1. Register of annual fire safety training.
2. Staff know how to use a fire extinguisher (interview)

### 7.2.6

Department/Unit Managers/OICs and team participate annually in mock fire drills. This is reflected in the organisation’s emergency fire plan which details what to do in case of a fire and all staff members have adequate knowledge of the plan.

1. There is a facility emergency fire plan (review plan).
2. Staff are aware of what to so in the case of the fire (interview).
3. There are mock fire drills (see records).

### 7.2.7

Department/Unit Managers/OICs and team ensure all rubbish, flammable materials are safely stored, safely handled and safely practiced.

1. There is safe storage of flammable material (observe, interview).

### 7.2.8

Department/Unit Managers/OICs and team comply with the organisation’s ‘No smoking / No Betel-nut policy’ and which is enforced.

1. There are no smoking signs around the facility premises.
2. There are no people smoking/chewing betelnut within the facility premises.

## 7.3 Plant, equipment, medical devices, supplies and consumables

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The Health Service Organisation should have processes for ensuring that its plant and equipment are chosen with consideration of function, safety, cost and effectiveness, and installed, operated and maintained according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

### 7.3.1

There should be correct and effective use of equipment via specialty training for staff where required.

1. Register of staff trained for specialised equipment (review training register).

### 7.3.2

There are efficient inventory practices, to ensure the ready availability of supplies and consumables.

1. A current inventory is maintained for medical supplies and consumables (review inventory).

### 7.3.3

The plant and equipment are chosen according to an assessment process that considers:    
compliance with relevant legislation, standards, guidelines and or codes of practice 
intended use and consumer needs
cost benefit
safety 
infection control
energy efficiency and environmental sustainability 
training needs, storage and distribution.

1. Evidence of an assessment process to procure equipment (review report).

### 7.3.4

The plant and equipment are being installed, tested and commissioned in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions and by appropriately qualified individuals.

1. Evidence that the manufacturer’s instructions were followed in the installation, testing and commissioning of equipment.
2. Evidence that installation, testing and commissioning of equipment were done by a qualified individual.

### 7.3.5

The plant and equipment should receive regular maintenance and cleaning according to a documented schedule with the following requirements:
recording of a log where required
monitored with respect to electrical, thermal radiant and mechanical hazards 
subject to documented processes for procurement, upgrading and replacement

1. There is a record of maintenance for equipment.
2. There is a record of equipment that requires upgrading, replacement and procurement.

### 7.3.6

Vehicles are subject to processes for procurement, operation, maintenance and safety that consider: 
government contracts, where relevant
vehicle operators
position descriptions that specify appropriate training and licences 
safe transport of patients
secure transport of health records, medications, equipment or supplies
workplace health and safety issues associated with vehicles, including appropriate seating, access and storage 
reporting of any vehicular accident as an incident.

1. Evidence of vehicle procurement records.
2. Evidence of requirements for vehicle operators.
3. Evidence of documentation of required training and licenses to operate the vehicle.
4. Records of patient transfer.
5. Records of transfer of health records, medications, equipment or supplies.
6. Inspection of vehicles for safety features (seating, seat belts etc.).
7. Incident reports for incidents involving vehicles.

## 7.4 Medical devices, supplies and consumables

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
Health Service Organisations can that demonstrate medical devices are installed, calibrated, maintained, repaired and decommissioned and disposed of by registered and/or properly qualified, trained and competent individuals. The term “medical device” applies to all equipment used in treatment, diagnostic activities, monitoring, and direct consumer / patient care.

### 7.4.1

Medical devices should be used safely with staff involved in the operation of medical devices undergoing necessary training and where necessary, correct licenses have been obtained.

1. Evidence that medical devices are installed, calibrated, maintained, repaired and decommissioned and disposed of by qualified individuals.
2. Medical devices are operated by staff who have received necessary training and/or licensing (see training records for equipment).
3. Evidence that clinical teams demonstrate safe use and maintenance of medical devices for patent monitoring including bedside monitors, telemetry monitors, etc. (observation)
4. Evidence that clinical teams demonstrate safe use and maintenance of devices for treatment: lasers, electrosurgery, diathermy, etc.
5. Evidence that pathology teams demonstrate safe use and maintenance of diagnostics devices for pathology, laboratory analysers, radiology equipment, endoscopes, etc.
6. Evidence that medical devices undergo calibration when necessary (review log).

### 7.4.2

Medical devices are cleaned, sterilised and recommissioned separately from general cleaning processes.

1. Evidence that the medical devices are cleaned and sterilised separately from general cleaning (observe).

### 7.4.3

“Supplies and consumables” are generally commodities with a shorter life such as dressings, syringes, disposable gloves, catheters, etc.

1. Evidence of policies for the procurement of medical supplies.
2. Evidence of policies for ordering, delivery and distribution of medical supplies.
3. Evidence of policies for the storage and inventory control of medical supplies.
4. Evidence of policies for the reuse of items.
5. Evidence of policies for the recall of items.

## 7.5 Preventive maintenance and cleaning

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The Health Service Organisations’ management of its assets, goods and general services should be supported by a well-planned and well-resourced system of preventive maintenance and cleaning.
Maintenance of buildings, plant and equipment should not be purely reactive: the organisation should implement a planned and coordinated system of preventive maintenance, to retain all assets in good working order, to extend the working life of critical and expensive equipment, and to reduce the risk associated with poorly operating equipment. All stages of preventive maintenance, from planning to completion, should be documented.

### 7.5.1

The Health Service Organisation can demonstrate preventive maintenance for its equipment so that the plant and the equipment within its Facilities operate safely and efficiently.

1. There is a schedule for preventive maintenance for equipment within the Facility.
2. If maintenance and cleaning cannot be carried out according to schedule, there is documentation of the reason (see maintenance log).

### 7.5.2

Cleaning of equipment should be done according to a documented schedule which describe the frequency of cleaning and the areas / items to be cleaned. It should also describe those areas / items not to be subject to general cleaning (e.g. medical devices).

1. There is a schedule for cleaning of items.
2. Barriers are in place when maintenance or cleaning is carried out.

### 7.5.3

Plant logs and maintenance processes should ensure that all buildings, including storage areas, plant, equipment, waste management areas and grounds are cleaned, maintained and serviced by qualified persons in accordance with infection control requirements, manufacturer specifications and relevant standards, including maintenance on high-risk plants such as cooling towers and pressure vessels.

1. Evidence that cleaning, maintenance of equipment, waste management areas and grounds are done by qualified personnel (interview, maintenance records).

## 7.6 Safety management

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The Health Service Organisation implements an organisation-wide system to create a safe environment and mitigate and manage risk; has guidelines address safety management consistent with relevant legislation, standards, guidelines and/or codes of practice, and include: workplace health and safety; suitability of the physical environment, manual tasks, radiation and laser safety, management of dangerous goods and hazardous substances and other identified high-risk practices.
Staff are involved in decisions that affect workplace health and safety and wellbeing and are trained in correct work practices to minimise the risk of injury. Procedures exist for the handling and use of dangerous goods and hazardous substances, such as;
availability of Safety Data Sheets
personal protective equipment (PPE) 
the environment in which dangerous goods and hazardous substances are to be stored and used
the limits of occupational exposure.

### 7.6.1

The clinical and non-clinical staff can demonstrate handling and management of hazardous material (biological, chemical, radiological, and/or physical), which has the potential to cause harm to humans, animals, or the environment.

1. There is proper signage or labels for hazardous materials.
2. Staff (clinical and non-clinical) know how to handle hazardous materials (observe and interview)
3. There is provision of necessary personal protective equipment, and other relevant equipment to handle hazardous material (observe).
4. Evidence that staff have access to Safety Data Sheets and other relevant resources include processes for managing a related emergency, such as a spill or an accidental exposure.
5. There is monitoring of compliance with safe practice requirements and remedial action taken where required (review of incident reports, complaints)
6. There is a designated radiation safety officer.

### 7.6.2

There is a radiation safety plan which has a register of all radioactive substances, procedures for the disposal of radioactive waste and radiation equipment.

1. The radiation safety plan is coordinated with external authorities (see meeting minutes or MoU)
2. There is a personal radiation monitoring system and monitoring of relevant rooms.
3. There are measures to keep patient radiation exposure to a minimum.
4. There are measures to keep staff radiation exposure to a minimum.

## 7.7 Asset management

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The Health Service Organisation has an asset register to document and maintain equipment, plant and biomedical devices. Asset management is a set of methods, processes and tools to optimise the “whole of life” value for the physical resources used to deliver health services. Physical asset or “fixed asset” mean land buildings, structures, medical equipment, static plant, transport, ICT, roads, parks and gardens.

### 7.7.1

There is a current asset register maintained by a designated officer.

1. Evidence of a current assets register (the condition, availability and performance).
2. Evidence of a designated officer to manage the assets register.
3. Evidence that new items are recorded in asset registration.
4. The asset register contains information on plants, equipment and biomedical devices.
5. There is an asset disposal strategy.

## 7.8 Security

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The Health Service Organisation performs security planning and management as an aspect of service planning, with resources allotted for security risk mitigation devices / strategies including (but not limited to);
the provision of onsite security staff
lighting in potential risk areas, such as car parks, access paths and storage facilities
 installation of locks and alarms, as appropriate
installation and monitoring of security cameras
installation of personal / duress alarms, with connection to the police or other appropriate response teams
installation of physical barriers, such as security glass
escape routes 
processes for restriction of access to certain areas for example, via swipe cards or code pads.
The Health Service Organisation also demonstrates support for a zero tolerance of violence or aggression toward all levels of staff through a commitment to education and training, and an effective response when incidents are reported.

### 7.8.1

Security measures are taken to ensure the protection of patients and staff from assault and loss of property; and the Facility from damage and loss.

1. Evidence of an organisation-wide system to identify major security and implement controls (meeting minutes or reports addressing security).
2. Evidence of Memorandum of Agreement for provision of security services if external services are used.
3. Evidence that potential high-risk areas emergency department, treatment – procedure rooms; plant rooms; pharmacies and drug storage areas, ATMs) have been considered in the planning of security measures (meeting minutes).
4. There is lighting in potential risk areas, such as car parks, access paths and storage facilities
5. There is installation and monitoring of security cameras
6. Evidence of installation of personal / duress alarms, with connection to the police or other appropriate response teams
7. Evidence of installation of physical barriers, such as security glass in specific areas
8. Evidence of escape routes.
9. Evidence of access restriction to certain areas using swipe cards or code pads.
10. Evidence that external service providers understand and comply with the organisation’s security controls and procedure (observe and interview)
11. There is evidence of steps taken to address “zero aggression” to all levels of staff (signages, training sessions)

## 7.9 Use of utilities

Source guidance (retained here; no database field):
The Health Service Organisation’s use of energy and water should be assessed annually, and efforts made to improve efficiency. Utilities are the basic services that the organisation uses to function, including water, power, ventilation, medical gases, and communications systems.

### 7.9.1

There is monitoring of water and electricity use, including:
How often is usage monitored? 
How has the organisation improved the efficiency of its energy and water usage?

1. Evidence of monitoring water and energy use (reports).
2. Evidence that there are efforts to improve efficiency (e.g. purchasing electrical equipment with a high energy rating, programmes to conserve energy and water -switching off equipment, good plumbing maintenance, etc.) (reports, interviews).
3. Evidence of documented contingency plans to address utility failures (see SOP or policy).
